Control-DINO: Feature Space Conditioning for Controllable Image-to-Video Diffusion

Edoardo A. Dominici1*, Thomas Deixelberger2,3*, Konstantinos Vardis1, Markus Steinberger2,3
*Equal contribution
Huawei Technologies 1Switzerland, 2Austria    3Graz University of Technology
Paper Code

TL;DR: A DINO-conditioned ControlNet for image-to-video diffusion that is appearance invariant offering semantic and structural control.


Appearance Decoupling

DINO features are prone to overfitting when naively used for ControlNet training, the model learns to reproduce training domain appearance rather than following structural guidance. We study this by dropping first frame conditioning and relying solely on text prompts. Setting the prompt to "blue" reveals the bias: baseline models ignore the prompt and reconstruct original colors, while our appearance-augmented training significantly reduces domain overfitting.

Appearance Invariant DINO ControlNet

We train on simple photometric and neural style augmentations, yet the model generalizes to unseen styles and relighting conditions, bounded only by the base model's generative capacity. At inference, a style transferred first frame (via IC Light, InstantStyle, Krea, etc.) anchors appearance while DINO features guide structure. We have experimentally found that training the diffusion model parameters improves quality, but makes it more difficult to combine with other ControlNets (e.g. point cloud or other 3D memory). We release a checkpoint with a Control-LoRA mechanism in the codebase.

Controlled Video Diffusion as Generative Rendering

We explore a range of 3D representations as conditioning sources for video generation. Voxel grids provide coarse but camera-consistent structural priors across views. Untextured mesh renderings capture surface geometry with holes and missing regions handled via visibility masking. Point clouds, whether from LiDAR scans or 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions, offer sparse but semantically rich guidance. Using Concerto, we extract DINO-like features directly from these uncolored representations, bypassing the need for RGB textures entirely. Each representation trades off spatial detail for semantic richness: the high dimensionality of foundation features (384 channels) compensates for coarse spatial resolution, enabling photorealistic video generation from geometry alone.
